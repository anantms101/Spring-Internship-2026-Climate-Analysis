Time-Series Analysis of Global Temperature Data
IDEAS - Spring Internship Program 2026 (Section 1)
This project conducts a comprehensive time-series analysis of historical global temperature anomalies from 1880 to 2016. The study focuses on data engineering techniques to identify long-term climate trends and seasonal variations.

**Project Overview**
Objective: To process, clean, and analyze climate records to visualize global warming patterns.

Dataset: monthly_csv.csv featuring 3,288 records from GCAG and GISTEMP sources.

Submission Date: February 2026.

**Key Technologies**
Python: Core programming.

Pandas & NumPy: Data manipulation and feature extraction.

Matplotlib: Data visualization for trend identification.

**Analysis Workflow**
Data Pre-processing: Conversion of dates to datetime objects and sorting.

Feature Engineering: Extraction of 'Year' and 'Month' attributes.

Moving Average: Implementation of a 12-month rolling window to smooth seasonal noise.

Trend Visualization: Yearly aggregation and monthly seasonality bar charts.

**Findings**
Confirmed a significant upward trend in global temperatures, peaking in 2016 with a yearly mean of 0.9644.

Identified October as the month with the highest historical mean temperature anomaly.
